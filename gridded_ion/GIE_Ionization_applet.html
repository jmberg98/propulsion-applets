Gridded Ion Engine I: Ionization

Electric Propulsion

In order for the ion engine to actually accelerate propellant to generate thrust, the propellant must be separated into positively and negatively charged particles, or ionized, in the ionization chamber. Neutral propellant particles are typically ionized by electron bombardment or radio-frequency (RF) wave emitters. Both deliver the required energy to separate an electron from a neutral particle, producing a positively charged ion that we can use for thrust!

Recall from the Ionization & Recombination Applet that electrons must have a certain amount of energy in order for a collision with a neutral particle to ionize it. Because of this, we want to maximize the path length the electron takes in the ionization chamber. This way, an electron has more chances to ionize a neutral atom. Consider a utilization fraction $\mu_u$: the fraction of propellant injected into the ionization chamber that actually gets ionized. The greater this utilization fraction is, the more efficient the engine!

Magnetic Confinement

Something you may have noticed from the simulation: only about 4–5% of the particles in the ionization chamber are ionized at any given time! However, the mass utilization fraction is fairly high, about 80%. How can this be?

Electron Energy Distribution

The voltage of the discharge circuit provides primary electrons the necessary energy to ionize neutrals. Recall however, that neutrals produce a positively charged ion and a secondary thermalized electron. Utilization Fraction

While the high tail of this distribution is able to ionize neutrals, the majority cannot. Instead, they travel about the chamber, transferring energy to other electrons until they are collected by the walls or reach sufficient ionization energy themselves.

High utilization fractions are achieved because the bulk chamber plasma forms a meniscus at each grid opening. From this meniscus (better seen in GIE II: Acceleration), the ions are extracted by the strong electric field. For neutrals to drift between the grids, they must do so by chance from their random motion. They may travel past the screen grid but hit the accelerator grid and get reflected back into the chamber. They may end up in the final exhaust jet. However, they are not nearly as favored to do so as the ions being extracted from the chamber plasma.

Conceptual Questions
Why are electrons that travel perpendicular into the magnetic field lines not trapped like in the Magnetic Mirror Applet?
Recall that in the derivation for magnetic mirror trapping, an important assumption was that the magnetic field strength was not changing during a cyclotron orbit. In a gridded ion engine, the magnetic field lines between cusps are not sufficient for this assumption to hold. Thus, incoming electrons are reflected or lost, not trapped.
