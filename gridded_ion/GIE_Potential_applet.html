Gridded Ion Engine IV: Potential Distribution

Electric Propulsion

All electric thrusters have a dedicated power processing unit (PPU) which drives how efficiently electrical energy from the spacecraft is transferred to the thruster’s circuits.1,3 Gridded ion engines require various power $P$ sources to operate due to having several circuits needed for the thruster to operate. Guiding Question

Recall that the power $P$ required for a circuit is equivalent to the product of its voltage difference $V$ and the current it is applied to, $\textcolor{#C060FF}{P} = I\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V}$. Knowing this, what circuit in the gridded ion engine consumes most of the power?

The Circuits
Circuit 1
The first circuit is between the electron-emitting “bombardment cathode” and the inside walls of the ion engine, which serve as the anode. Electrons emitted from the cathode collide into neutral particles, possibly ionizing them, releasing an ion and a secondary electron. The primary and secondary electrons then travel about the chamber until they are collected by the anode walls, completing the circuit. Power $P$ is needed to lower the electric potential $\phi$ of the emitted electrons. This way, when electrons start to enter the plasma of the ionization chamber, they gain electric potential, gaining kinetic energy. This provides more ionization energy to the primary electrons, making it easier to ionize neutral particles.
Circuit 2
The second circuit is between the screen and accelerating grids toward the ion engine exit. The circuit creates a large voltage difference $V$ across a very small distance, and thus creates a strong electric field $\vec{E}$. This strong electric field extracts ions from the bulk plasma upstream, causing them to accelerate up to tens of kilometers per second. Because no current is collected or emitted, and $\textcolor{#C060FF}{P} = I\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V}$, no power is consumed by this circuit.
Circuit 3
The third circuit is between the anode walls of the thruster and the neutralizer cathode that emits electrons to join the outgoing beam of ions. In order to properly neutralize the ion beam, the neutralizer cathode must match the current of the outgoing beam. Additionally, the potential difference $V$ must match the net voltage drop of the ions. Because the circuit must apply a large voltage drop to a large electron current, the neutralizer or beam supply consumes most of the power $P$ in the gridded ion engine.

The Voltages

$V_{discharge}$: Provides electrons the necessary energy to drop in electric potential $\phi$, allowing them to gain kinetic energy as their potential raises to the bulk plasma potential $V_{net}$. Ions born from these ionizing electrons are at the bulk plasma potential $V_{net}$.

$V_{neutralizer}$: Provides electrons the necessary energy to drop in electric potential $\phi$ to 0 V, allowing them to join the ion jet downstream. Consumes the bulk of the thruster’s power $P$, due to needing to provide a large voltage drop to a large current of electrons.

$V_{total}$: The voltage drop $V$ between the screen and accelerator grid. Dictates the space charge limit (GIE II: Acceleration) and thus mass flow rate of the thruster.

$V_{accel}$: Set at (not recommended) or below the plasma beam’s potential $\phi$ to prevent electrons from “back-streaming” into the grid. $V_{accel}$ adversely affects $I_{sp}$ and thrust density $F/A$, so caution how high it is set.4

Notice that both the ion and electron start and end at the same potential $\phi$ but go through different paths to do so.

From Potential to Force

The electric field $\vec{E}$ and potential $\phi$ are related by a work-energy relation.2 For a given electric potential difference $V$ applied over a greater distance, there is a weaker electric field. Likewise, for a given electric potential difference applied over a smaller distance, there is a stronger electric field. Notice that the electric field lines point in the direction of decreasing electric potential $\phi$. Ions, which are positively charged are attracted to lower electric potential $\phi$, while electrons, which are negatively charged are attracted to higher electric potential $\phi$. This is because the electric force $F_e$ is the electric field $\vec{E}$ times the particle’s charge $q$, both of which are signed quantities.

Axial Field and the Force It Exerts $$ \boxed{\;\textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz}\;} \qquad\qquad \boxed{\;\textcolor{#FF4400}{F_z} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\,\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz}\;} $$
Show derivation
Connect the electric field $\vec{E}$ and electric potential $\phi$ or $V$ by energy balance. Work Done on Charge = Change in Electric Potential Energy $$ -\!\int \textcolor{#FF4400}{F_e}\,dz = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V} $$ $$ -\!\int \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}\,dz = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V} $$ Use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus $$ -\cancel{\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E} = \cancel{\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{V}}{dz} $$ Solve for $E$ $$ \boxed{\; \textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{V}}{dz} = -\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz} \;} $$ Solve for $F$ $$ \textcolor{#FF4400}{F_e} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E} $$ $$ \boxed{\; \textcolor{#FF4400}{F_z} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\,\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz} \;} $$ The field is the slope of the potential; the force is that slope times the charge.
