Circuit Diagrams

There are several circuits in gridded ion engines. Each circuit must be closed to prevent the spacecraft from accumulating charge. In a given circuit, the current out must equal the current in and the sum of voltage rises must equal the sum of voltage drops. See if you can identify each of the circuits above!

The Circuits
Circuit 1
The first circuit is between the electron-emitting “bombardment cathode” and the inside walls of the ion engine, which serve as the anode. When an electron is emitted from the bombardment cathode, it will travel throughout the chamber until it eventually touches the wall and is collected, completing the circuit. If an electron hits a neutral particle and ionizes it, the reaction results in a positively charged ion and two electrons. The newly added “secondary” electron can also be collected by the walls. In fact, the ion may hit the walls by chance, losing its charge and becoming neutral again.
Circuit 2
The second circuit is between the screen and accelerating grids toward the ion engine exit. The circuit creates a voltage difference between the grids, accelerating any ions that happen to drift in between the two. However, there is an ion jet, a form of current, leaving the grids, meaning the circuit isn’t quite closed (yet).
Circuit 3
The third circuit is between the neutralizer cathode, anode walls, and outgoing ion jet. The neutralizer cathode emits the collected electrons in the ionization chamber, expelling them toward the ion jet and completing Circuit 2.

From Potential to Force

The electric field and potential are related by a work-energy relation. For a given electric potential difference applied over a greater distance, there is a weaker electric field. Likewise, for a given electric potential difference applied over a smaller distance, there is a stronger electric field. Notice that the electric field lines point in the direction of decreasing electric potential. Ions, which are positively charged are attracted to lower electric potential, while electrons, which are negatively charged are attracted to higher electric potential. This is because the electric force is the electric field times the particle’s charge, both of which are signed quantities.

Axial Field and the Force It Exerts $$ \boxed{\;\textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz}\;} \qquad\qquad \boxed{\;\textcolor{#FF4400}{F_z} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\,\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz}\;} $$
Show derivation
Connect the electric field $\vec{E}$ and electric potential $\phi$ or $V$ by energy balance. Work done on charge = Change in electric potential energy $$ -\!\int \textcolor{#FF4400}{F_e}\,dz = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V} $$ $$ -\!\int \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}\,dz = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V} $$ Use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus $$ -\cancel{\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E} = \cancel{\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{V}}{dz} $$ Solve for $E$ $$ \boxed{\; \textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{V}}{dz} = -\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz} \;} $$ Solve for $F$ $$ \textcolor{#FF4400}{F_e} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E} $$ $$ \boxed{\; \textcolor{#FF4400}{F_z} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{E_z} = -\,\textcolor{#C060FF}{q}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#F5C518}{\phi}}{dz} \;} $$
The field is the slope of the potential; the force is that slope times the charge.
