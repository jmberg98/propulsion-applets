Gridded Ion Engine II: Acceleration

Electric Propulsion

Now that most of the neutral propellant particles have been ionized, they are ready to be accelerated to produce useful thrust! A large voltage difference is applied over the short distance between the screen and accelerator grids, creating a strong electric field $E = V_T/d$. Much like chemical rockets, only so many particles can be accelerated at a time via the accelerator grid. This means that for a given gridded ion engine size, there is a limit to the amount of thrust that can be generated. Let’s explore what the limit seems to be.

Guiding Questions

The more ions that pass through the accelerator grids, the greater our mass flow rate and thus our thrust. What causes the fundamental limit on the number of ions that pass through the screen grid into the accelerator grid?

Just downstream of the accelerator grid, electrons from the neutralizer join the exhaust jet to neutralize it. These electrons are attracted to the ions coming out of the accelerator grid openings. Why might we want to implement a small voltage rise just downstream of the accelerator grid, knowing this would lower thrust and $I_{sp}$?

Conceptual Questions
If we have both positively charged ions and negatively charged electrons available to us, why do we always choose to accelerate the ions and not the electrons?
Recall that electrons have far less mass than ions, by three to four orders of magnitude. While they can be accelerated extremely fast, their minuscule mass means that the thrust an electron jet produces is near zero.
The larger $V_T/d$ (the electric field between the screen and accelerating grids) is, the higher thrust density we can achieve. Why can’t $V_T/d$ be arbitrarily high?
$V_T$ is determined by the desired mission and thus desired exhaust velocity or specific impulse, as related by $v_{ex} = \sqrt{2qV_T/m}$. Thus, to maximize $V_T/d$, we want to minimize $d$, the distance between the screen and accelerator grids. However, if $d$ is too small, then the risk of the screen–accelerator grid circuit shorting is high. This is especially true after vibrations during launch, as grids might shift, causing a short. Additionally, recall that $V_T/d$ is the electric field strength between the screen and accelerator grids. If $E$-field is too strong, then breakdown can occur: creating electrical arcs between the two grids, damaging them.
Child–Langmuir Law

The current density in between the screen and accelerator grid is limited by the Child–Langmuir Law. If too many positively charged ions exist between the two grids, the next ion to be extracted by the grid field would be repelled by the amount of ions in between the grids.

Thrust Density

The thrust density is important because it helps us determine the approximate thrust of our device and approximate how well the device would perform at different sizes. For example, a large thrust density is optimal for a miniature thruster, as these thrusters have smaller exit areas.
