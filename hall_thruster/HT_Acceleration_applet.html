Hall Thruster II: Acceleration

Electric Propulsion

In the gridded ion engine, the accelerating gap had to be kept empty of electrons, so the only charge in it was the ion charge being accelerated. That charge pushes back on the ions still arriving, and the Child–Langmuir space-charge limit is the ceiling it imposes.

Color key thrust ion charge q, electron B field E field neutral

A Hall thruster accelerates its ions in a region that is full of electrons: the buzzsaw from the previous page sits in the same volume the ions are accelerating through. The plasma there is quasineutral, the net space charge is close to zero, and the limit that capped the gridded engine never appears.1 This one difference is most of why Hall thrusters displaced gridded engines commercially.

The gridded curve is the same Child–Langmuir result derived in GIE II, $F/A = \tfrac{8}{9}\varepsilon_0 V^2/d^2$, so the two pages cannot disagree. The Hall curve carries no such term: with electrons filling the accelerating region there is no net space charge to push back, and thrust density is set by whatever ion current the discharge sustains, $F/A = j\sqrt{2 m_i V / q}$. Read it two ways. Compare each engine at the voltage it actually runs, the two marked points, and the Hall thruster is about an order of magnitude denser: 21 against 1.5 newtons per square meter. Compare them at the same voltage and the gap is far wider still, because the space-charge ceiling falls as $V^2$ and punishes the low-voltage end hardest. That same $V^2$ is why the gridded ceiling stops binding at high voltage, where the Hall curve is only climbing as $\sqrt{V}$, and it is why gridded engines keep the high-specific impulse end of the market.

Filling the accelerating region with electrons is what removed the ceiling. It also handed those electrons a road back to the anode.
What a Collision Does to a Magnetized Electron

Every electron in the channel ends on the anode eventually, whether the field likes it or not: the cathode emits them, the discharge circuit pumps them, and the anode is the only positive surface in the machine. The question is never whether they arrive but how long they take, because a slow electron ionizes many neutrals on the way and a fast one is just current the power supply paid full discharge voltage for and got no thrust from. So start with how an electron crosses a plasma at all.

With no magnetic field, drop the electron inertia and keep the two forces that matter, the pressure gradient pushing the population outward and collisional drag holding it back.4

Momentum balance, no field $$ \nabla p_e \;\approx\; -\,n_e m_e \nu_e \vec{v}_e \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad n_e \vec{v}_e \;\approx\; -\,\frac{kT_e}{m_e \nu_e}\nabla n_e $$ Fick's law, with a diffusivity $\textcolor{#ffe94d}{D_e} = kT_e / m_e\nu_e$. The collision frequency sits in the denominator, which is where everybody expects it. Each collision is a step taken backward, a more collisional plasma diffuses more slowly, and an electron that never collides crosses at its thermal speed. Hold on to that intuition for one more equation, because the field is about to invert it.

Now switch the field on, perpendicular to the gradient. The magnetic force joins the balance, and solving for the flux gives a diffusivity across the field that is the old one divided by the square of a single dimensionless number.

Cross-field diffusivity, and the Hall parameter $$ \boxed{\;\textcolor{#ffe94d}{D_\perp} \;=\; \frac{\textcolor{#ffe94d}{D_e}}{1+\beta^2}\;} \qquad\qquad \beta \;=\; \frac{\omega_{ce}}{\nu_e} \;=\; \frac{e\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}}{m_e \nu_e} $$ $\beta$ is the Hall parameter: gyrations completed per collision.
The algebra

Add the magnetic force to the balance: $kT_e\nabla n_e \approx -n_e m_e \nu_e \vec{v}_e - e n_e \vec{v}_e \times \vec{B}$.

That is one equation in two unknowns, $\vec{v}_e$ and $\vec{v}_e \times \vec{B}$. To get a second, cross the whole thing with $\vec{B}$ and use $\vec{v}\times\vec{B}\times\vec{B} = -\vec{v}_\perp B^2$, which holds because the flux we are solving for is perpendicular to $\vec{B}$: $kT_e\nabla n_e \times \vec{B} \approx -n_e m_e \nu_e\, \vec{v}_e\times\vec{B} + e n_e \vec{v}_e B^2$.

Eliminate $\vec{v}_e \times \vec{B}$ between the two and the flux comes out as one term along $-\nabla n_e$ and one perpendicular to both $\vec{B}$ and $\nabla n_e$:

$$ n_e\vec{v}_e \;\approx\; \frac{-D_e\nabla n_e \;-\; \vec\beta \times D_e \nabla n_e}{1+\beta^2} $$

The second term is the $\nabla p \times \vec{B}$ drift, which carries particles around the gradient rather than down it and moves nothing toward the anode. The first term is the one that does, and it is ordinary diffusion with $D_e$ cut by $1+\beta^2$. Putting the electric field back in is a substitution rather than a rederivation: replace $\nabla n_e$ with $\nabla n_e + (en_e/kT_e)\vec{E}$ throughout, and the same denominator now also governs the conductivity.

In a Hall thruster $\beta$ is in the hundreds, so the $1$ is not worth keeping, and what is left is worth reading twice.

Strongly magnetized limit, $\beta \gg 1$ $$ \textcolor{#ffe94d}{D_\perp} \;\approx\; \frac{\textcolor{#ffe94d}{D_e}}{\beta^2} \;=\; \frac{kT_e}{m_e}\frac{\nu_e}{\omega_{ce}^2} \;=\; \frac{m_e kT_e}{e^2}\,\frac{\nu_e}{\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}^2} $$ The collision frequency has moved to the numerator. Across a magnetic field, collisions no longer impede diffusion, they are the diffusion: a collisionless magnetized electron goes nowhere at all. The sign of the whole dependence flipped when the field came on, and every loss on this page follows from that one reversal.

The picture behind the algebra is worth having in mind, because the applet below draws it directly. Between collisions the electron is locked onto the $\vec{E}\times\vec{B}$ orbit from the previous page and makes no axial progress whatsoever. A collision breaks the orbit and lets the guiding center jump about one Larmor radius, and because the axial electric field is pushing on the electron the whole time, those jumps are biased toward the anode. Axial transport is not something that survives the collisions. It is something that only exists because of them. At SPT-100 conditions, counting real collisions only, the two diffusivities are six orders of magnitude apart, $D_e \approx 2.8\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{m^2/s}$ against $D_\perp \approx 0.6\ \mathrm{m^2/s}$, and spreading the electrons one centimeter across the field takes tens of microseconds instead of tens of picoseconds.4

The Leak Current, and What Sets It

Turning that into a current takes one step. The mean axial speed an electron makes toward the anode is the azimuthal drift speed $\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}/\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}$ scaled down by the fraction of a gyration that a collision interrupts, and the collisions that matter are with the heavy particles, mostly the neutrals still streaming out of the anode manifold.

Cross-field electron speed and the leak it carries $$ v_{e_x} \;\approx\; \frac{\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}}{\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}}\, \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\omega_{ce}} \qquad\qquad \textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}} \;=\; e\,n_e\,v_{e_x}\,A_\mathrm{ch} \qquad\qquad \nu_{eH} \;=\; \textcolor{#9aa3b2}{n_H}\,\bar{c}_e\,Q_{eH} $$ $\nu_{eH}/\omega_{ce}$ is $1/\beta$: one over the gyrations completed between collisions.

Those electrons reach the anode having been accelerated through the full discharge voltage and having pushed on nothing. What they cost is written as the anode efficiency, the fraction of the discharge current that actually left as beam. Comparing the two currents needs a speed to compare against, and the usual choice is that the beam ions cross the magnetic barrier at roughly half their final speed.

Anode efficiency $$ \frac{\textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}}{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}} \;=\; \frac{v_{e_x}}{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}/2} \;=\; \frac{2}{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}}\frac{\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}}{\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}} \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\omega_{ce}} \qquad\qquad \boxed{\;\eta_a \;=\; \frac{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}}{\textcolor{#F5C518}{I_a}} \;=\; \frac{1}{1 + \textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}/\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}}\;} $$ Wall losses ignored, so the discharge current is just $\textcolor{#F5C518}{I_a} = \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B} + \textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}$.

Now push the mass flow rate up, which is the one lever that raises thrust density on hardware you have already built. The neutral density in the channel rises with it, so $\nu_{eH}$ rises with it. The plasma density rises with it too, since every ion in the acceleration region came from the ionization region upstream, so $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}$ rises in proportion. But $\textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}$ carries both factors, $n_e$ and $\nu_{eH}$, and so it rises as the square. The ratio of the two therefore grows linearly with flow: the thrust density keeps climbing, and the fraction of the discharge current doing the pushing keeps falling.

That is enough to put a number on the ceiling. Ask for a leak no worse than $\textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}/\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B} \le 0.2$ and the inequality above becomes a limit on the collision frequency, hence on the neutral density, hence on the flow.

The flow ceiling, in the form it is usually quoted $$ \nu_{eH} \;\le\; 0.1\,\frac{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}\,\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}\,\omega_{ce}} {\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \textcolor{#9aa3b2}{n_H} \;=\; \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\bar{c}_e Q_{eH}} \;\le\; 3.5\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}} $$ SPT-100 numbers: $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i} = 16\,000$ m/s, $\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B} \approx 0.02$ T, $\omega_{ce} \approx 3.5\times10^{9}$ rad/s, $\textcolor{#F5C518}{E} \approx 5$ kV/m, $T_e \approx 10$ eV, $Q_{eH} \approx 3\times10^{-19}$ m².4

Flown thrusters run several times below that density, which is the interesting part: on this argument alone there is room to run an SPT-100 at several times its nominal flow and collect several times the thrust from the same hardware. That high-flow regime has been proposed as an alternative to magnetoplasmadynamic thrusters where tens of kilowatts are available, and it is largely unexplored, in part because facilities that can absorb that much power are rare.4

Faster Than the Collisions Allow

One honest complication, and it is a large one. Measured electrons cross the field considerably faster than any count of real collisions predicts. The accepted explanation is that they scatter off small-scale electrostatic fluctuations in the plasma, which are always present, and which act like collisions without being any. Bohm's empirical form for the resulting diffusivity, and the tunable version everyone actually fits data with, are

Anomalous, or Bohm, diffusion $$ D_\mathrm{Bohm} = \frac{kT_e}{16\,e\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}} \qquad\qquad D_\mathrm{anom} = \frac{kT_e}{c_B\,e\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}} \qquad\qquad \nu_\mathrm{anom} = \frac{\omega_{ce}}{c_B} $$ $c_B \sim 16$ to $100$. Written as a collision frequency it simply adds: $\nu_e + \nu_\mathrm{anom}$ goes into the same $D_\perp$.

Two things follow, and the applet below has a switch for both. First, the anomalous term scales as $\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}^{-1}$ rather than $\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}^{-2}$, so it wins at high field, and the experiments confirm the $\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}^{-1}$ slope. Second, and more useful here, it does not depend on the mass flow at all: it sets the offset, and the real collisions set the slope. Switch it off in the figure and you get the clean textbook scaling with an anode efficiency near $0.95$; switch it on at $c_B \approx 100$ and the number lands near $0.75$, which is what an SPT-100 actually does. The same fluctuations are why cross-field transport is the hardest thing to predict in a Hall thruster, and they are the reason a code cannot be written from first principles and trusted.

What to look for: follow the electron with the white ring, the one whose trail is drawn several seconds deep. It runs almost straight down, lap after lap around the annulus, and each lap sits a little further left than the one before. The downward part is free and gets it nowhere; every pixel of leftward progress it makes, it made in a collision. The eight faint ones behind it are doing the same thing, and how far apart they have drifted is the spread that $D_\perp$ measures. The frame is the acceleration region with the channel cut along one radius and unrolled: anode at the left edge, exit plane at the right, and the top and bottom edges are the same cut, which is why an electron leaving the bottom returns at the top. The magnetic field points out of the page and the electric field points right, so the drift runs down the frame and every electron in the figure is on the same buzzsaw. Each mark is one scattering event, and each one jumps the orbit about one Larmor radius in a random direction with a bias toward the anode, which is the random walk that $D_\perp$ describes and the drift that $v_{e_x}$ describes, drawn as the same motion, because they are. The white marks are real collisions and land on the neutral that was hit; the violet ones are anomalous scatters off a plasma fluctuation, with nothing there to hit, and they are drawn only when the switch for them is on. The electron wearing a white ring is the one being traced, and the eight faint ones are drawn faint so that it can be followed. Nothing separates them physically: all nine run the same equations. The grey neutrals are drawn standing still because on this timescale they are: they cross the channel at a few hundred meters per second against an electron drift of a few hundred thousand. Take the flow up and the frame fills with neutrals, the marks come faster, and the walk visibly speeds up: that is the whole argument of this page in one slider. Switch anomalous scattering off if the walk looks too busy to follow. With it on, the fluctuations scatter an electron tens of times per lap and the trail is a continuous jitter, which is both what a real thruster does and too fast to count; with it off, the hops separate and you can watch a single one happen. Take the field up instead and the walk slows steeply, since the jump length falls as $1/\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}$ and the number of jumps needed to cross rises as $\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}^2$. Drag on the plot to move the operating point; the crossing marked on it is where the collisional leak reaches the customary fifth of the beam current, and the model puts it at a neutral density of $3.5\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, the same number the inequality above gives, which is the one check on the arithmetic worth stating. $\eta_a$ returns on the potential page as one factor of three in the thrust efficiency; this figure is where the factor comes from.

Born Across a Range of Potentials

There is a second consequence worth separating out. In a gridded engine every ion starts at essentially the same potential, the screen grid, and falls through essentially the same voltage. In a Hall thruster an ion is created wherever its parent neutral happened to be ionized, which is spread over the length of the acceleration region, so different ions fall through different fractions of the total. The exhaust therefore leaves with a spread of speeds rather than one speed.
