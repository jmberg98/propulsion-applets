Hall Thruster II: Acceleration

Electric Propulsion

In the gridded ion engine, the accelerating gap had to be kept empty of electrons, so the only charge in it was the ion charge being accelerated. That charge pushes back on the ions still arriving, and the Child–Langmuir space-charge limit is the ceiling it imposes.

Color key thrust ion charge q, electron B field E field neutral

A Hall thruster accelerates its ions in a region that is full of electrons: the buzzsaw from the previous page sits in the same volume the ions are accelerating through. The plasma there is quasineutral, the net space charge is close to zero, and the limit that capped the gridded engine never appears.1 This one difference is most of why Hall thrusters displaced gridded engines commercially.

The gridded curve is the same Child–Langmuir result derived in GIE II, $F/A = \tfrac{8}{9}\varepsilon_0 V^2/d^2$, so the two pages cannot disagree. The Hall curve carries no such term: with electrons filling the accelerating region there is no net space charge to push back, and thrust density is set by whatever ion current the discharge sustains, $F/A = j\sqrt{2 m_i V / q}$. Read it two ways. Compare each engine at the voltage it actually runs, the two marked points, and the Hall thruster is about an order of magnitude denser: 21 against 1.5 newtons per square meter. Compare them at the same voltage and the gap is far wider still, because the space-charge ceiling falls as $V^2$ and punishes the low-voltage end hardest. That same $V^2$ is why the gridded ceiling stops binding at high voltage, where the Hall curve is only climbing as $\sqrt{V}$, and it is why gridded engines keep the high-specific impulse end of the market.

Born Across a Range of Potentials

There is a second consequence worth separating out. In a gridded engine every ion starts at essentially the same potential, the screen grid, and falls through essentially the same voltage. In a Hall thruster an ion is created wherever its parent neutral happened to be ionized, which is spread over the length of the acceleration region, so different ions fall through different fractions of the total. The exhaust therefore leaves with a spread of speeds rather than one speed.
