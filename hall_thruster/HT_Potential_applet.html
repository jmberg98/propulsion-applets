Hall Thruster IV: Potential Distribution

Electric Propulsion

Everything so far has pointed at one question. The gridded engine's potential profile could be read straight off its hardware: every rise and drop sat on a grid or an electrode you could point to. A Hall thruster's potential falls in open space, in the last few millimeters before the exit plane, with nothing there to mark the spot.

Color key thrust ion charge q, electron B field E field neutral

What puts it there is the magnetic field. Electrons move along a field line almost freely and across one only with difficulty, which makes a field line very nearly an equipotential. Shape B and you have shaped φ: the steep axial gradient forms where the radial field is strongest, and designers put that peak just inside the exit plane.2 This is the sense in which a Hall thruster's accelerator grid is made of magnetic field.

Drag the peak and the whole acceleration region goes with it. The link between the two panels is the one assumption this figure makes: electrons cross the field with a mobility that falls as $1/B^2$, so holding their current fixed puts the axial field where crossing is hardest, $E_z \propto B_r^2$, and $\varphi$ is that field integrated back from the exit. Pull the peak upstream and more of the fall happens inside the channel, where the ions it produces are still close enough to the walls to hit them. Push it downstream and the beam is born outside the channel, more divergent and less well confined. Where designers actually put it, just inside the exit plane, is the settlement between those two.

Circuit Diagram
Trace one loop

Four supplies, and only one of them is in the ion's path. The discharge supply does all of the accelerating work, and notice that half of its loop is not wire at all: the return leg is electrons streaming back through the plasma from the cathode. The keeper and the heater only keep the cathode itself running. The magnet supply touches neither electrode, carries none of the discharge current, and is still the one that decides where the potential falls, because it sets B. That is the loop this page has been closing.
