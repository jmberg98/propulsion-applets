Hall Thruster IV: Potential & Field Distribution

Electric Propulsion

Not every ion ejected from a Hall thruster is expelled at the same exhaust velocity. Because the electric potential varies along the discharge channel, and because neutrals are ionized at various points along the channel, newly formed ions experience different potential drops through the channel. Because of this, it is important to understand where ionization and acceleration occur in the thruster.

The electric potential and radial magnetic field distributions of the thruster are very closely linked. Before the peak magnetic field strength, ions and electrons are both free moving. Even though the anode is set at a high potential, the ions cannot experience a potential drop due to the shielding provided by the nearby, mobile electrons. As the ion drifts toward the peak $B_r$ strength, however, a greater number of the electrons are trapped in the Hall current's magnetic mirrors. The electrons can no longer shield the ions from the anode voltage. Most of the ions have already been or are just ionized from the electron ring and incoming electrons, so they accelerate into the exhaust plume.

The Distributions on the Hardware

There are fewer power supplies in a Hall thruster than in a gridded ion engine. The one primary power source of a Hall thruster is for the anode discharge voltage, as a large current of electrons must be pumped through a large potential drop to neutralize outgoing ions and feed the azimuthal Hall current.

Power Supply

At the same time, there are various components of efficiency to be considered in a Hall thruster.

Mass Utilization
$$ \boxed{\;\eta_u = \frac{\textcolor{#FF4400}{\dot m_i}}{\textcolor{#FF4400}{\dot m}}\;} $$

As in a gridded ion engine, the mass utilization is the fraction of mass flow rate that results in a mass flow rate of ions out of the engine. This is important because only ions can be accelerated to the desired exhaust speed required for the mission.

Backstreaming

The more electron current directed toward the ion beam as opposed to leaking to the anode, the more efficient the anode. Power is wasted on electrons that do not neutralize ions. Additionally, power is wasted on electrons that backstream and yet do not ionize any neutral particles.

Energy Efficiency

Not all ions are born before or at the potential drop of the thruster, some are born afterward. These constitute a loss. As the fraction of ions formed before or at the potential drop increases, so does the energy efficiency of the thruster.

The total efficiency of the thruster is the product of all three.

$$ \boxed{\;\eta \;=\; \eta_u\,\eta_a\,\eta_E\;} $$

Electrons that cross the magnetic barrier and reach the anode were pumped through the full discharge voltage and pushed on nothing. The anode efficiency is the fraction of the discharge current that actually left as beam.4

Cross-Field Electron Speed When the scattering collision frequency $\nu_{eH}$ with heavy particles becomes comparable to the cyclotron frequency $\omega_{ce}$, electrons can no longer maintain pure azimuthal drift: each collision lets the electric field nudge them toward the anode by about one Larmor radius, at a velocity similar to the azimuthal drift velocity. $$ v_{e_x} \;\approx\; \frac{\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}}{\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}}\, \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\omega_{ce}} \qquad\qquad \nu_{eH} = \textcolor{#9aa3b2}{n_H}\,\bar{c}_e\,Q_{eH} $$ $\nu_{eH}/\omega_{ce}$ is $1/\beta$: one over the gyrations completed between collisions. Leak Current $$ \textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}} = \textcolor{#C060FF}{e}\,n_e\,\frac{\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}}{\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}} \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\omega_{ce}} $$ The electron density $n_e$ in the acceleration region is governed by the flow of ions from the upstream ionization region, so it increases in the same proportion as $\dot m$ or $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}$. But $\textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}$ carries both $n_e$ and $\nu_{eH}$, so it rises as the square: the ratio of the two grows linearly with flow.
Current Balance, Ignoring Wall Losses $$ \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B} = \textcolor{#F5C518}{I_a} - \textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \eta_a = 1 - \frac{\textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}}{\textcolor{#F5C518}{I_a}} = \frac{1}{1 + \textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}/\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}} $$ The Flow Ceiling This Implies Assume the ions constituting $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}$ cross the magnetic barrier at about half their eventual speed $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}$, and ask for a leak no worse than a fifth of the beam: $$ \frac{\textcolor{#ffe94d}{I_\mathrm{leak}}}{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{I_B}} = \frac{v_{e_x}}{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}/2} = \frac{2}{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}}\frac{\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}}{\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}} \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\omega_{ce}} \;\le\; 0.2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \nu_{eH} \;\le\; 0.1\,\frac{\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}\,\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B}\,\omega_{ce}} {\textcolor{#F5C518}{E}} $$ $$ \textcolor{#9aa3b2}{n_H} = \frac{\nu_{eH}}{\bar{c}_e Q_{eH}} \;\le\; 3.5\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}} $$ SPT-100 numbers: $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i} = 16\,000$ m/s, $\textcolor{#00DDB5}{B} \approx 0.02$ T, $\omega_{ce} \approx 3.5\times10^{9}$ rad/s, $\textcolor{#F5C518}{E} \approx 5$ kV/m, $T_e \approx 10$ eV, $Q_{eH} \approx 3\times10^{-19}$ m². Measured neutral densities in this type of thruster are still significantly lower than this value.4
Higher electron collisionality hurts efficiency, and raising $B$ (where feasible) is the way to counteract it. This is the same $B^{-2}$ confinement the diffusion derivation gives, read as a current rather than as a diffusivity.
Energy Efficiency Factor

An ion is created wherever its parent neutral happened to be ionized, so the exhaust does not leave at one energy. It leaves with a distribution of birth potentials $f(\varphi)$, and $f$ is not a second thing to be measured: it is the ionization rate along the channel pushed through the potential profile above, since $f(\varphi)\,d\varphi = \dot n_i(z)\,dz$ with $\varphi = \varphi(z)$.

Energy Efficiency $$ \boxed{\;\eta_E = \left(\int_0^1 \sqrt{\varphi}\,f(\varphi)\,d\varphi\right)^{\!2}\;} $$
Show derivation
Exit Velocity of an Ion Born at Potential $\varphi$ $\varphi$ is the birth potential normalized on the discharge voltage, so it runs from $0$ to $1$ and every factor below is at most one. $$ \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}(\varphi) = \sqrt{\frac{2\textcolor{#C060FF}{e}\,\varphi\,\textcolor{#F5C518}{V_d}}{\textcolor{#FF4400}{m_i}}} = \sqrt{\varphi}\;\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_{i,\max}} $$ Thrust Is the First Moment, Power Is the Second Thrust goes as the mean of $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}$ over the distribution, while the beam power goes as the mean of $\textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}^2$, which is just $\varphi$ itself: $$ \left\langle \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i} \right\rangle = \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_{i,\max}}\int_0^1 \sqrt{\varphi}\,f(\varphi)\,d\varphi \qquad \left\langle \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}^{2} \right\rangle = \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_{i,\max}}^{2}\int_0^1 \varphi\,f(\varphi)\,d\varphi $$ The Efficiency Is the Ratio of the Two A thruster is judged on the thrust it makes for the power it spends, so the penalty for a spread is the square of the mean against the mean of the square: $$ \eta_E = \frac{\left\langle \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i} \right\rangle^{2}} {\left\langle \textcolor{#3DBFFF}{v_i}^{2} \right\rangle} \cdot \int_0^1 \varphi\,f(\varphi)\,d\varphi \;=\; \left(\int_0^1 \sqrt{\varphi}\,f(\varphi)\,d\varphi\right)^{\!2} $$
If every ion were born at the top of the fall, $f$ would be a spike at $\varphi = 1$, the integral would be one, and $\eta_E$ would cost nothing. That is what a gridded engine gets from starting every ion at the screen grid, and it is what a Hall thruster gives up in exchange for escaping the space-charge limit.
