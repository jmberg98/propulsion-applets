Hall Thruster III: Neutralization

Electric Propulsion

The argument for neutralizing the beam is the same one the gridded engine made. Charge is conserved: expel positive ions and do nothing about it, and the spacecraft is left negatively charged, which endangers its electronics and eventually pulls the exhaust back, canceling thrust.

Color key thrust ion charge q, electron B field E field neutral

What is different here is the hardware doing it. The gridded engine carried a neutralizer cathode whose only job was the beam. A Hall thruster has one hollow cathode mounted outside the body, and it does two jobs at once: it supplies the electrons that stream back into the channel to sustain the discharge and feed the buzzsaw, and it supplies the electrons that neutralize the beam.1

Cathode Coupling Voltage

Getting electrons from the cathode into the plasma is not free. The cathode sits at a potential somewhat below the plasma it is feeding, and that difference, the cathode coupling voltage, is voltage the ions never get to fall through.1 It is small next to the discharge voltage but it is not negligible, and it is one reason the beam energy does not equal the discharge voltage.
