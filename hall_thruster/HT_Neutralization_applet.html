Hall Thruster III: The Cathode

Electric Propulsion

The cathode serves two roles: it provides the required electron current to neutralize the outgoing beam of ions (just like the gridded ion engine), and ionizes the neutral propellant particles injected into the discharge channel. Recall that the exhaust jet must leave quasineutral or it will eventually travel back to the spacecraft. The cathode can be configured either externally, like a gridded ion engine, or it can be placed coaxially in the center of the thruster.

Neutralization

A significant portion of the cathode current enters the thruster as leak current $I_\mathrm{leak}$. This provides the electrons necessary to form the Hall current. The electrons that escape their magnetic mirrors additionally have a chance to accelerate upstream toward higher potential, giving them greater energy and thus a higher chance to ionize a neutral. Thus, while these backstreaming electrons are a loss mechanism, they help ionize neutrals and ensure that the power spent on their potential drop was not wasted. This helps improve both mass utilization and energy efficiency respectively.
